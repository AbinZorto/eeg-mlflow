This study evaluated a sparse low-montage resting-state EEG framework for identifying clinically interpretable features associated with remission outcomes in a home-based transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) randomised sham-controlled trial in adults with major depressive disorder. Using 4-channel eyes-closed EEG, we conducted a controlled sweep of 150 successful runs across two model families, an advanced hybrid 1D CNN-LSTM and a linear support vector machine (SVM), varying window size (2--10 s) and per-fold feature budget (\texttt{inner-k} 1--70) with leave-one-participant-out group equalization and SMOTE during training.

The strongest configuration (hybrid model, 6 s windows, \texttt{inner-k=1}) achieved patient-level ROC-AUC 0.816, PR-AUC 0.579, balanced accuracy 0.786, accuracy 0.810, and MCC 0.571 over 21 held-out participants. The permutation test confirmed a significant advantage over chance (raw $p=0.008$), which survives Bonferroni correction for the two model-family comparisons ($p_\text{corr}=0.016$). As robustness checks, a max-T permutation (10,000 iterations across all 150 sweep configurations) yielded $p_\text{corr}=0.302$, and the full-sweep Bonferroni was mechanically uninformative ($p_\text{corr}=1.00$) given the 150-test multiplier; we report all three tiers. The best SVM configuration (8 s, \texttt{inner-k=30}) reached ROC-AUC 0.510 and MCC 0.277 ($p=0.469$, uncorrected). Notably, the best hybrid performance was obtained with extreme sparsity, selecting 1 feature per fold and yielding only 7 unique features in at least one fold. Hard-set overlap across folds was modest (mean pairwise Jaccard 0.190; Kuncheva index 0.187), while recurrent features showed perfect mean effect-direction consistency.

These findings suggest that patient-level discrimination signals may be recoverable from highly sparse representations of low-montage EEG, although statistical significance does not survive correction for the full sweep. The strongest run concentrated its signal in a small recurrent feature set centered on temporal spectral entropy and frontal-temporal difference measures. The results converge with previous power spectral density-based findings and support low-montage resting-state EEG as a basis for further pretreatment stratification research, pending independent validation.
